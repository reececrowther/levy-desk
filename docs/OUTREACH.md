# Levy Desk: outreach plan to first paying users

**Written:** 7 November 2026
**Window:** now until the Edinburgh Q4 return deadline (30 January 2027) and Glasgow's levy start (25 January 2027)
**Goal by 31 January 2027:** 5 paying accounts, at least 2 of them letting agents. (A guess at what is realistic for one person part-time. Adjust once you see reply rates.)

---

## 1. Positioning: who actually needs this

Be honest about the competition first:

- **visitorlevy.scot** (the official portal) has its own free levy calculator.
- **Airbnb** offers a tool to add the levy to prices; **Bookster** and other channel managers can add it to bookings.
- A host with **one property and a handful of bookings** can do the return with a spreadsheet. They are the hardest to charge.

Where Levy Desk wins is **volume and repetition**:

- Many properties, many bookings, several platforms (Airbnb + Booking.com + direct).
- Several **clients** (owners) whose figures must stay separate, with owner statements.
- Rules that trip people up: cleaning fees, the 5-night cap with one-off fees, VAT, cross-quarter stays, pre-October-2025 bookings.
- **A second council arriving** (Glasgow, January 2027) with different rules.

So the primary customer is the **letting agent / short-let management company** with roughly 10 to 200 Edinburgh units. Secondary: owners with 3+ units and B&Bs/guest houses.

**One-line pitch:** "Upload your Airbnb and Booking.com exports and get each client's Edinburgh levy figures, checked against the council's guidance, ready for visitorlevy.scot."

## 3. Channels, ranked

### A. Direct email to Edinburgh short-let management companies (main channel)

- **Who:** companies managing short lets in Edinburgh. Start with independents (EdinBnB-type local specialists); the big national operators (Houst, Pass the Keys, GuestReady, UpperKey) likely build in-house, so treat them as a later, longer shot.
- **How to build the list (aim for 60 to 100):** Google Maps and search ("short term let management Edinburgh", "Airbnb management Leith", etc.), ASSC member directory, Edinburgh letting agents with a holiday-let arm. Record: company, website, contact name, role, email, approx. units, source, date contacted.
- **Legal (PECR):** you can cold-email **limited companies, LLPs and Scottish partnerships** without prior consent, if you identify yourself and include a working opt-out. **Sole traders and ordinary English partnerships count as individuals**: do not cold-email them. Keep a do-not-contact list. Do **not** use the council's short-term let licence register to email individual licence holders.
- **Cadence:** 10 to 15 personalised emails a day, 3 days a week. One follow-up after 4 working days, one more after 10. Then stop.
- **Market size (found 9 Nov 2026):** Edinburgh has only about 35 to 40 independent management companies reachable by email; the tracker now holds 46 Edinburgh and Glasgow prospects. Edinburgh alone will not reach 60 to 100, so Glasgow agents, multi-unit operators and accountants make up the rest.
- **Expect:** reply rate maybe 5 to 15% for a timely, specific, non-salesy email (low confidence, ~50%). From 80 emails, that is roughly 4 to 12 conversations.

### B. Accountants and bookkeepers who serve hosts (partner channel)

- One accountant can bring 10+ clients, and they are fielding levy questions right now.
- Offer: free accountant access (already in the product), a referral discount for their clients, and a short "how Levy Desk calculates" sheet they can check.
- 20 to 30 targeted emails to Edinburgh/Lothians firms that mention hospitality, holiday lets or landlords.

### C. Communities (slow, but compounding)

- ASSC, Edinburgh host Facebook groups, Airbnb Community Centre UK, r/AirBnBUK, landlord forums.
- **Answer questions properly first.** Link the calculator or a guide page only when it genuinely answers the question. Check each group's rules on self-promotion.
- Good topics right now: "Do I include cleaning fees?", "Does Booking.com pay the levy?", "Which quarter does a New Year stay go in?", "Do I pay VAT on the levy to the council?". You now have council-referenced answers to all four.

### D. Existing calculator leads (cheapest)

- The CalculatorLead table already holds people who asked for a reminder. Send a short, useful January deadline email in early January with the four gotchas above and a link to sign up. (They consented to a reminder: keep it a reminder, not a hard sell.)

### E. Not now

- Paid ads: small, seasonal market and unproven conversion. Revisit once you know what converts.
- Big national operators: long sales cycles.

## 6. Discovery questions (by email, no calls)

Ask two or three of these in your reply to anyone who responds, not all at once. These decide what phase 3 should be.

1. How did you do the October return? Who did it, and how long did it take?
2. How many clients/units are in Edinburgh? Any in Glasgow?
3. Which platforms and channel manager do you use? Can you export bookings?
4. Do owners ask you for levy statements?
5. What would make you trust a tool's figures?
6. What would you pay to never think about this again? (Then show pricing.)
7. Who else should I talk to?

## 8. Open decisions for Reece

- **Pricing for single-unit owners:** free for one property means most individual hosts never pay. Options: keep it (as a funnel) or make the free tier calculator-only and charge from the first property for return figures.
- **Done-for-you data handling:** if you import clients' exports yourself, you need a short data-processing statement and a deletion habit.
- **How much time per week** you can give this: the timeline assumes about 6 to 8 hours.
